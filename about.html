The working relationship

Good conversations.
Better decisions.

The way we work together matters as much as the tasks on the roadmap. Here is what I want that relationship to feel like.

You can ask why.

A recommendation should have a reason you can understand. I want the trade-offs and assumptions to be visible, including the reasons to leave something alone.

You know what needs your input.

Good work needs access, approvals and context. We identify the decisions that need your team and the work I can take forward.

We can change our minds.

When the evidence changes, the plan should be allowed to change with it. A useful review ends with a decision, not just another report.

Questions I come back to

Curiosity, with
a practical purpose.

These questions help me connect the details of search and content to the business they are meant to serve.

01

Why would a customer choose this?

Before optimizing a page, understand the offer. What does it help someone do, and what makes it worth their attention?

02

Can the team actually implement it?

A recommendation has to fit real capacity, access and priorities. A smaller plan that gets delivered can be more useful than a perfect document.

03

What would change our decision?

Define what you would need to see before continuing, stopping or adjusting the work. That makes review time more useful.
